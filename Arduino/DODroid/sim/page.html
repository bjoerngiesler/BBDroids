Model and assumptions

Pitch plane with three degrees of freedom: the wheel (both halves, rolling without slip), the chassis as a pendulum hanging off the axle, and the head assembly (neck, head and head counterweight) pivoting about the main axle, driven by the neck servo relative to the chassis. Yaw follows from the torque difference of the two wheels. Sideways tipping is not simulated; the readout compares the lateral acceleration with what the track width can hold.

The 3D view shows your CAD model (DOv2Evo), reduced to about 39,000 triangles: the wheel halves, the chassis, the neck on the main axle and the head on its servos; the lead block and tyres are drawn as simple shapes. Masses and geometry come from the CAD (JamesB D-OEvo V3): wheel r = 126 mm, track 100 mm, chassis 3.9 kg including 2.5 kg of lead with its centre of mass 57 mm below the axle. Printed parts are assumed at 0.8 g/cm³. The head assembly is assumed to be balanced about the axle by its counterweight; its inertia about the axle (0.022 kg·m²) comes from the CAD plus an estimate for servos and electronics.

Firmware 1:1: balance controller (P on IMU pitch with ±1° error deadband, reversed), DOVelControlOutput (ramp, rotation, max speed), two wheel speed PIDs on the encoders (wheel relative to chassis), motor deadband, automatic switch to position hold (auto_pos) when the sticks are idle for 0.5 s, neck and head animation in stepHead(). IMU sign and neck direction are chosen so that the controller damps and the neck leans forward when driving; both are adjustable.
